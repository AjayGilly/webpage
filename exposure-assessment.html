What we look for

Attackers start with reconnaissance.
So should you.

An external exposure assessment looks at your organisation from the outside in — no agents, no credentials, no disruption. Here's what typically turns up.

Attack surface

The full inventory of internet-facing assets tied to your organisation, including the ones no one has owned for years.

IP ranges · ASNs · hosting providers · shadow IT

Open ports and services

Management interfaces, databases and remote access protocols that are reachable from the public internet.

RDP · SSH · SMB · RPC · exposed admin panels

Misconfigurations

Default credentials, directory listings, verbose error pages and debug endpoints left switched on in production.

Open buckets · exposed .git · index-of · stack traces

Certificate and TLS health

Expired or expiring certificates, deprecated protocol versions and weak cipher suites still being negotiated.

TLS 1.0/1.1 · self-signed · expiry within 30 days

Subdomains and DNS

Forgotten hosts, staging environments and dangling records pointing at infrastructure you no longer control.

Subdomain takeover · orphaned CNAMEs · zone hygiene

Email security posture

SPF, DKIM and DMARC records that allow your domain to be spoofed in business email compromise attempts.

DMARC p=none · missing SPF · unaligned DKIM

Cloud exposure

Public storage, unauthenticated APIs and workloads spun up outside the change process across your cloud estate.

AWS · Azure · GCP · public endpoints and buckets

Known vulnerabilities

Public-facing software matched against known CVEs, weighted by whether the flaw is actively being exploited.

CVE mapping · KEV catalogue · EPSS scoring
